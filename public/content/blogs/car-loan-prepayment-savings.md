Car loans come with higher interest rates compared to home loans, yet the vehicles depreciate quickly. Is it always a good idea to pay off the loan early? Let's analyze.

## The Truth About Car Depreciation

Unlike a house, which normally appreciates in value over time, a car depreciates rapidly. As soon as you drive a new car off the lot, it loses around 10% of its value. By the end of the first year, it may have lost 20%, and after five years, it could be worth only 40% of what you paid for it.

If you take a 7-year car loan, you might eventually owe more than the car is worth (being "underwater" on your loan). This makes fast repayment highly desirable.

## Should You Prepay Your Car Loan?

The quick answer is: **Usually, yes.** Because auto loans have relatively high interest rates (often 8% to 15%), prepaying yields a guaranteed "return" equal to that interest rate.

- **Higher Interest Relief:** Auto loans aren't tax-deductible (unless for business), so there's no reason to hold onto the debt.
- **Better Cash Flow:** Eliminating a car payment frees up hundreds of dollars/rupees per month.
- **Avoiding Negative Equity:** By paying down the principal quickly, you ensure you can always sell the car for more than you owe.

## Beware of Prepayment Penalties

Before you write a large check to your lender, check your loan agreement for a prepayment penalty or an early closure fee. Some banks charge 1% to 3% of the outstanding loan amount if you pay it off early within the first year or two.

> **⚠️ Warning: Rule of 78**
> Some older or private loans use the "Rule of 78," front-loading the interest so heavily that prepaying in the later years offers almost no savings. Always ask your bank if interest is calculated on a daily/monthly reducing balance.

## When NOT to Prepay?

If you secured a promotional 0% or extremely low interest rate (e.g., 2% to 4%) from the manufacturer, there is no financial benefit to paying the loan off early. You are better off investing that extra cash into a high-yield savings account or the stock market, where it can earn 5%+ returns.
