For many Americans, the mortgage interest deduction is one of the most significant tax breaks available. However, since the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA), the rules have changed. Here’s what you need to know to navigate US home loan taxes.

## 1. Mortgage Interest Deduction

You can deduct the interest paid on mortgage debt used to buy, build, or substantially improve your main home or a second home.

- **Debt Limit:** You can deduct interest on up to **$750,000** of mortgage debt ($375,000 if married filing separately).
- **Grandfathered Loans:** If your mortgage was taken before December 16, 2017, the limit remains at the older $1 million threshold.
- **Itemizing is Key:** This deduction only applies if you choose to itemize your deductions rather than taking the Standard Deduction.

## 2. SALT Deduction (State and Local Taxes)

The SALT deduction allows you to deduct property taxes paid at the state and local levels.

- **Combined Limit:** The total deduction for state and local income taxes (or sales taxes) plus property taxes is capped at **$10,000** ($5,000 if married filing separately).
- **Impact:** In high-tax states, homeowners often hit this limit quickly, making it essential to calculate whether itemizing still provides a better return than the standard deduction.

## 3. Federal Housing Administration (FHA) Loans

The FHA provides specialized loans designed for low-to-moderate income borrowers.

- **Low Down Payment:** You can buy a home with as little as 3.5% down.
- **Credit Flexibility:** Easier credit requirements than traditional conventional loans.
- **USDA Loans:** For those in eligible rural areas, USDA loans offer **0% down payment** options, making home ownership accessible without significant upfront savings.

## 4. Home Office Deduction

If you are self-employed and use a portion of your home exclusively for business, you may be eligible to deduct a percentage of your mortgage interest, property taxes, and insurance as business expenses.

## 4. Mortgage Insurance Premiums (PMI)

While historically deductible, the deduction for private mortgage insurance (PMI) often expires or requires legislative renewal. Always check the current year's IRS guidelines to see if your PMI payments are eligible.

### Conclusion

Tax laws in the US are complex and subject to change. While the mortgage interest deduction remains a powerful tool for homeowners, the higher standard deduction means fewer people itemize today.
