Prepaying a loan isn't just about dumping cash whenever you have it; it's a strategic math problem. Doing it right can save you decades of debt. Let's look at the optimal strategies.

## The Compounding Nature of Debt

Just as compound interest works _for_ you in investing, it works _against_ you in borrowing. In the early years of a 20-year or 30-year loan, up to 80% of your EMI goes entirely toward paying the interest. Your principal barely moves.

This makes the first 5 years of a loan the most critical timeframe. Any principal you can knock out early halts decades of compounding interest forming on that amount.

## Strategy 1: The One Extra EMI Rule

This is the simplest and most famous strategy. Instead of paying 12 EMIs a year, configure your budget to pay 13.

- **How to do it:** Divide your monthly EMI by 12, and add that amount to every monthly payment. Alternatively, take your annual bonus and make a lump sum payment equivalent to one EMI.
- **The Impact:** For a 20-year home loan at 9%, making just one extra EMI per year can drop your total tenure to under 16 years.

## Strategy 2: The 5% Annual Increase

Assuming your salary will increase annually to beat inflation, your EMI should increase as well. If you increase your EMI payout by just 5% every year, the results are staggering.

If your EMI is ₹50,000 this year, bump it up to ₹52,500 next year (a simple ₹2,500 increase). A 20-year loan will be cleared in just over 12 years if you consistently apply a 5% step-up.

## Strategy 3: Bonus Dumping

If your job yields an annual performance bonus or stock payout, commit to putting 50% of the net bonus toward your loan principal immediately upon receiving it. Let the remaining 50% go toward your lifestyle or investments.

> **🎯 Reduce Tenure vs. Reduce EMI?**
> When you make a prepayment, your bank will usually offer two options: lower your monthly EMI, or shorten your loan tenure. **Always choose to shorten the tenure** if you want to save the most interest. Lowering the EMI feels nice monthly, but strings the interest out over a longer period.
