Australia has one of the unique property tax systems in the world, heavily favoring investors through various deduction schemes. If you own or plan to buy property in Australia, understanding these terms is vital.

## 1. Negative Gearing

Negative gearing occurs when the cost of owning an investment property (interest and expenses) exceeds the rental income it generates.

- **Tax Benefit:** The resulting net loss can be used to **offset your other taxable income**, such as your salary. This reduces your overall tax bill significantly.
- **Long-Term Play:** While you lose money on a cash-flow basis monthly, the goal is for the capital growth of the property to far exceed these losses over time.

## 2. Capital Works Deduction (Division 43)

You can claim a deduction for the "wear and tear" on the structure of your building.

- **Rate:** Usually **2.5% per year** for up to 40 years.
- **Scope:** This includes the cost of construction, extensions, and structural improvements (like a new driveway or patio).

## 3. First Home Guarantee (FHG) & Help to Buy

The Australian government helps first-time buyers bypass the expensive Lenders Mortgage Insurance (LMI).

- **First Home Guarantee:** Eligible buyers can purchase a home with as little as a **5% deposit** without paying LMI, as the government guarantees the rest.
- **Help to Buy Scheme:** A shared equity scheme where the government contributes up to 40% of the purchase price for a new home (30% for existing homes), significantly lowering the loan amount and monthly EMI.

## 4. Plant and Equipment Depreciation (Division 40)

For newer properties (built after 2017), you can also depreciate removable items like carpets, blinds, and air conditioners. This is a non-cash deduction that helps improve your tax position.

## 4. First Home Buyer Grants (FHOG)

Each Australian state offers different versions of the FHOG. Typically, if you are building or buying a brand-new home, you may receive a one-off grant of **$10,000 to $30,000** to help with your deposit or costs.
