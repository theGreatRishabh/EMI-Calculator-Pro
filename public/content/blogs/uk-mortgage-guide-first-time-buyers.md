The UK property market can be challenging, but the government offers several paths to help homeowners manage costs. From savings bonuses to tax-free rental income, here is your guide to UK-specific property benefits.

## 1. Lifetime ISA (LISA) for First-Time Buyers

The Lifetime ISA is a powerful tool for those saving for their first home.

- **Bonus:** The government adds a **25% bonus** to your savings, up to a maximum of £1,000 per year.
- **Limit:** You can save up to £4,000 annually.
- **Condition:** The property must cost £450,000 or less, and you must have had the account open for at least 12 months.

## 2. Rent-a-Room Relief

If you decide to let out a furnished room in your main home, you can take advantage of the Rent-a-Room Scheme.

- **Tax-Free Limit:** You can earn up to **£7,500 per year** tax-free from lodgers.
- **Automatic:** If your rental income is below this threshold, the tax exemption is automatic. If it's higher, you only pay tax on the portion above £7,500.

## 3. First Homes Scheme & Shared Ownership

The UK government also provides direct support for those who cannot afford a full mortgage.

- **First Homes Scheme:** Offers first-time buyers and key workers a discount of **at least 30%** on the market value of a new home.
- **Shared Ownership:** Allows you to buy a share of a home (between 10% and 75%) and pay rent on the remaining part. This is an excellent way to get onto the property ladder with a smaller deposit.

## 4. Stamp Duty Relief (England & Northern Ireland)

First-time buyers in the UK benefit from reduced Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT).

- **Current Threshold:** No stamp duty is paid on the first £425,000 of a property priced up to £625,000.
- **Savings:** This can save new homeowners thousands of pounds in upfront costs.

## 4. Mortgage Interest Tax Relief (Buy-to-Let)

If you are an investor, be aware of "Section 24" changes. Individual landlords can no longer deduct mortgage interest from rental income before paying tax. Instead, you receive a **20% tax credit** on your mortgage interest.
