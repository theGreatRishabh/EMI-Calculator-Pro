Home loans are long-term commitments, often spanning 15-20 years. However, you don't have to be stuck with the same high EMI forever. Here are the top 5 strategies to bring that number down.

## 1. Home Loan Balance Transfer (Refinancing)

If your current bank is charging a higher interest rate than the market average, consider a **Balance Transfer**.

- **The Rule of Thumb:** If you find a lender offering a rate at least **0.50% lower** than your current rate, refinancing usually pays for itself even after processing fees.

## 2. Negotiate with Your Existing Lender

Banks often offer better rates to new customers. If you have a **Credit Score above 750** and a flawless repayment history, ask your bank to "Reset" your interest rate to match current market offers. They might charge a small conversion fee, but the long-term savings are worth it.

## 3. Opt for EMI Redux After Prepayment

When you make a lump-sum prepayment, most banks automatically reduce your **Tenure** (keeping EMI the same). However, you can specifically request the bank to **Reduce the EMI** instead while keeping the tenure constant. This immediately increases your monthly disposable income.

## 4. Periodic Incremental EMIs

Instead of a fixed EMI, consider increasing your EMI by **5% every year** as your salary grows. While this doesn't reduce your _current_ EMI, it drastically reduces your total interest and tenure, meaning you pay "Zero EMI" much sooner.

## 5. Switch to a Floating Interest Rate

If you are currently on a Fixed Rate, you might be paying a premium for "certainty." In an environment where interest rates are stable or falling (as predicted for late 2026), switching to a **Floating Rate** can lead to immediate EMI reductions as the repo rate dips.
